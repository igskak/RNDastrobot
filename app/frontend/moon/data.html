How the times are calculated

Each moment is the exact instant at which the ecliptic longitude of the Moon equals that of the Sun (new moon) or differs from it by 180 degrees (full moon). The longitudes are apparent geocentric positions from the Swiss Ephemeris by Astrodienst, which is built on the planetary ephemerides of NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory.

The instant is found by halving a six-hour interval until the difference is far smaller than a second, then cut to the whole second. Times are in UTC, so there is no daylight saving or time-zone ambiguity. The sign is the tropical zodiac sign of the Moon's longitude at that instant, with signs starting at the March equinox point, and the degree is the whole degree inside the sign.

How we checked them

On 9 November 2026 we compared all 29 dates in the table with an independent calculation made with the JPL DE421 ephemeris through the Skyfield library. The largest difference was 0.6 seconds, which is the second we cut off. We did not compare them with a calendar or an almanac: the check is against the ephemeris the others are built on.

Using the dates and sending corrections

The dates are facts and free to use. A link back to this page is appreciated, in print too. If you compare them with another source and find a difference of more than a minute, tell us through the contact on the terms page and say which date and which source. We check it and publish the outcome here.
