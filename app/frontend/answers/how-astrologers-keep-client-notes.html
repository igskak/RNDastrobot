How do astrologers keep client notes?

The four methods in use, what each one survives, and the part of a reading that notes never catch.

Four ways: a paper notebook, the notes field inside chart software, a general notes app or spreadsheet, or a practice workspace that keeps the notes beside the charts and a recording of the session itself.

A word on wording before the detail. People search for "client notes", so that is the question as asked. Inside a practice the thing being kept is softer than that: what you and the person you read for talked about, what they told you, what you said would matter in March. The record exists so that the next conversation can start where the last one ended.

Nothing in that table says paper is wrong. A notebook is the fastest capture tool ever made and plenty of excellent astrologers will use one for another thirty years. What it cannot do is be in two places, be searched, or tell you what you said rather than what you remembered to write.

The thing notes cannot do

Here is the honest limit of any notes field, including ours. During a reading you are talking. You are watching a chart, following where the person goes, choosing what to say next. Whatever you write down is a thin summary of a two-hour conversation, written by someone who was busy. Written afterwards, it is a thin summary written by someone who is tired.

Astrologers already know this, which is why so many of them tell the person they read for to record the session. The advice is almost part of the craft. But look at where that recording ends up: on their phone. You gave the reading, and six months later when they book again, you are the one working from memory.

So the version of this job worth solving is not "keep better notes". It is: the substance of the session should still be here, on the right person's profile, the next time you open it. In Steliara the consultation runs as a video call in the app, the audio is recorded once both sides agree, it is transcribed, and a short summary is saved on that person's profile. Should astrologers record their consultations instead of taking notes?

Recording solves the problem notes cannot: you were talking, not typing, so your notes are a thin summary of a two-hour reading written by someone who was busy. A recording with a transcript and a short summary gives you what was actually said. Recording needs the agreement of the person you read for, and a reason to keep it.

Is the recording the client makes on their phone enough?

It solves their problem, not yours. That file belongs to them, has no transcript and no summary, is attached to no chart, and in practice you will never ask for a copy. The person who most needs the history before the next session is the astrologer.

How long should an astrologer keep client records?

Long enough to prepare for a follow-up, and no longer than you can justify.
