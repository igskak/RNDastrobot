What is the best astrology software for a professional practice?

Three questions decide it. Here they are, with five named tools, their prices, and where each one is the right answer.

There is no single best one. Pick on three things: the traditions you work in, the platform you work on, and whether you need the consultation itself kept. Depth favors Solar Fire, breadth of technique favors Astrolium, and kept consultations favor Steliara.

Every roundup that names one winner is either selling that winner or was written by someone who has never run a practice. The useful version of this answer is a set of questions you can answer about your own week.

Question one: how deep does your technique go?

If your work lives in horary, electional search, primary directions, Vedic dasas and nakshatras, or astrocartography, this question settles it before the others are asked. Solar Fire V9 lists 30 house systems, interpretation reports, full mapping and Vedic dasas for $360 as a one-time purchase. Astrolium lists 23 house systems on its free tier, and its roadmap marks a horary workspace, electional search, primary directions, astrocartography and Jyotish with vargas and dashas as live today. TimePassages ships written interpretations built in.

Steliara is deliberately narrower here. Six house systems are selectable in its interface, there is no astrocartography, no Vedic dasas or nakshatras, and no written interpretation reports, because the assistant finds facts and does not interpret. If your technique needs any of that, buy for technique and stop reading.

Question two: what are you working on?

This one is short. Solar Fire is Windows only: its maker states V9 runs on Microsoft-supported versions of Windows including 8, 10 and 11, and points Mac users at a page about running Windows programs on a Mac. Astro Gold is the depth option elsewhere, with macOS, Windows, iPad, phone and Android versions on astrogold.io. TimePassages covers Windows, macOS, mobile and a browser version. Astrolium, Astrologer, Chronos and Steliara are browser tools and run anywhere.

The honest cost of a browser tool is the other side of the same coin: Steliara has no offline mode, because it is a cloud app. On a train with no signal, a desktop program wins.

Question three: does the consultation have to survive?

This is the question most comparison pages skip, and for a practice it is the expensive one. A reading runs one to two hours. Months later the person books again, and the astrologer is the one reconstructing it: what did we cover, what did they tell me, which transit were we watching and did I say spring or autumn.

On 12 October 2026 none of the public pages read for this page described recording or transcribing a consultation: Solar Fire's feature page, astrogold.io, the TimePassages editions page, the Chronos home page, the Astrolium pricing page and the Astrologer home page. Astrolium's own roadmap is worth singling out, because it lists what is live and what is coming, and recording appears on it in no state at all. That is an absence on a page today, not a promise about what any of them will ship. Astrologer does lead with booking and Google Calendar sync, so if your painful part is scheduling rather than memory, it is the better pick. Chronos books consultations, but with its own astrologers.

Steliara runs the reading as a video call in the app, records the audio once both sides agree, transcribes it, and saves a short summary on that person's profile beside their charts and your notes. The audio is kept six months and then deleted automatically; the transcript and summary stay. That is the longer version of this page.

Where Steliara is the wrong choice

Worth having in one place, because a comparison that finds nothing wrong with itself is an advert. Steliara does not have: astrocartography, Vedic dasas or nakshatras, horary or electional search, primary directions, time lords, written interpretation reports, a chart-art designer, an offline mode, a self-serve data export, or a free tier. Six house systems are selectable, against 30 in Solar Fire and 23 in Astrolium. The export gap in particular is worth weighing before you move a practice: getting a copy of your data is a request under the privacy policy, not a button in the app.

And one more that is nobody's fault: session history does not migrate. Charts come across from ZET, Astro.com, Astrolog and the .SFcht files Solar Fire and Astro Gold both use, up to 2,000 records per file with duplicates flagged. Recordings and notes made in other tools do not.

AI will never read a chart for you. It just finds the facts faster: the aspects, the exact dates, where a planet sits. The summary captures what you said. You stay the astrologer.

Questions

Which astrology software is best on a Mac?

Astro Gold for depth, since astrogold.io sells a macOS version alongside Windows, iPad, phone and Android. Any browser workspace for the rest, including Astrolium, Astrologer, Chronos and Steliara. Solar Fire is the one to rule out: its maker's support page points Mac users at running Windows programs under emulation.

Is the most expensive astrology software the best?

No. Solar Fire V9 is $360 once and TimePassages reaches $699 for its Complete Package, while Astrolium starts free and Steliara starts at $24 a month. Price tracks calculation depth and licence model, not how well a tool fits a practice. A $360 calculator that leaves your session history in three other places can cost more time than it saves.

What does a professional astrology tool cost per month?

Subscriptions run from free to about $79 a month. Astrolium publishes Free, $11, $29 and $79 plans plus a $12 personal plan. Steliara is $24 a month for Practitioner and $39 for Studio, or $20 and $32 billed annually, with a 14-day trial of every feature and no card. Desktop programs are one-time purchases instead: Solar Fire V9 at $360, TimePassages from $79 to $699.

Where is Steliara the wrong choice?

If you need many house systems, astrocartography, Vedic dasas and nakshatras, horary or electional search, written interpretation reports, an offline mode, a one-click data export or a free tier, Steliara does not have them today. Six house systems are selectable in its interface against Solar Fire's 30 and Astrolium's 23.

How accurate are the calculations?

Steliara computes on the Swiss Ephemeris, the same engine most professional programs use, with an orb table tuned with a practising astrologer. Tropical and sidereal zodiacs are both available, with five ayanamshas.

Can I try these before paying?

Steliara gives every account a 14-day trial with every feature unlocked and no card, and a lapsed account turns read-only rather than locking you out of your own charts. Astrolium publishes a free tier. Chronos advertises a seven-day free trial. Desktop programs are generally bought outright, so look for the maker's demo version.
